Understanding your relationship with alcohol

Use this page to reflect on patterns and prepare questions for a qualified healthcare professional. It does not determine whether you have a disorder or provide a treatment plan.

Withdrawal can be dangerous. Stopping alcohol abruptly after regular heavy use can cause severe complications, including seizures. Speak with a doctor before stopping or sharply reducing if you drink heavily or daily. Seek emergency care for seizures, hallucinations, severe confusion, loss of consciousness, or severe shaking.

Patterns worth noticing

Alcohol use exists on a spectrum. A useful starting point is whether it affects health, relationships, work, finances, safety, or the ability to follow your own intentions.

Possible signs of reduced control

  • Drinking more or longer than intended
  • Wanting to cut back but repeatedly finding it difficult
  • Spending significant time drinking or recovering
  • Continuing despite health, relationship, work, or safety problems
  • Needing more alcohol to experience the same effect
  • Experiencing withdrawal symptoms when reducing or stopping

Common triggers

  • Social gatherings and celebrations
  • Stress, boredom, loneliness, or difficult emotions
  • Specific times, places, routines, or people
  • Conflict or pressure
  • Being around others who are drinking

Questions for self-reflection

  • Do I often drink more than I planned?
  • Has someone I trust expressed concern?
  • Do I rely on alcohol to relax, sleep, or cope?
  • Has drinking affected driving, safety, work, money, or relationships?
  • What happened during previous attempts to reduce?

When professional support is especially important

Consider speaking with a doctor or qualified addiction professional when drinking affects safety or responsibilities, withdrawal may be possible, you have relevant medical or mental-health conditions, or previous attempts to reduce have not been successful.

Emergency warning signs

Seek urgent medical care for seizures, hallucinations, severe confusion, loss of consciousness, severe shaking, breathing difficulty, chest pain, or another immediate medical concern.
