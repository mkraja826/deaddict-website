Understanding your relationship with alcohol

Whether you're wondering if your drinking has become a problem, actively trying to cut back, or supporting someone else, this page can help you understand patterns and next steps.

Stopping alcohol abruptly after regular heavy use can be medically dangerous, including risk of seizures. If you drink daily or heavily, speak with a doctor before stopping or significantly reducing. If you experience confusion, seizures, hallucinations, or severe shaking, seek emergency medical care immediately.

What this looks like

Alcohol use exists on a spectrum, from occasional social drinking to patterns that affect health, relationships, work, or safety. It's common to feel uncertain about where your own drinking falls on that spectrum — that uncertainty is a reasonable starting point, not something to have already figured out.

Common signs of loss of control

  • Drinking more or longer than intended
  • Wanting to cut back but finding it difficult
  • Spending a lot of time drinking or recovering from it
  • Continuing despite it causing problems at home or work
  • Needing more to feel the same effect

Common triggers

  • Social gatherings and celebrations
  • Stress, boredom, or loneliness
  • Certain times of day or locations (e.g., after work)
  • Difficult emotions or conflict
  • Being around others who are drinking

Potential effects on daily life

Sleep quality, energy, mood, finances, and relationships are commonly affected. Effects vary widely between people — this list is meant to help you reflect, not to predict your experience.

Self-reflection questions

  • Has anyone close to me expressed concern about my drinking?
  • Do I feel like I need a drink to relax or cope?
  • What would change in my life if I drank less?
  • Have I tried to cut back before? What happened?

How DeAddict may help

You may consider using DeAddict's daily check-ins to log when and why you drink, identify recurring triggers, and set a gradual reduction or quit-date goal with reminders and grounding exercises for difficult moments.

When to consider professional help

You may consider speaking with a qualified professional if drinking is affecting your health, relationships, safety, or ability to meet responsibilities, or if you've tried to cut back on your own without success. A doctor can also advise on whether medical support is needed to stop safely.

Emergency warning signs

Seek emergency medical care for seizures, hallucinations, severe confusion, very rapid heartbeat, or loss of consciousness during withdrawal.

Recommended tools

  • Daily drink & urge log
  • Trigger pattern report
  • Gradual reduction plan

Healthy replacements

  • Alcohol-free social routines
  • Evening walk or exercise
  • A grounding breathing exercise
